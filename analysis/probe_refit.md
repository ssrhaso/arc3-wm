# Corrected latent-probe analysis (2026-09-05)

Regenerated by `scripts/probe_refit.py` over the Phase-6 dumped latents, replacing the
CI-separation test in `scripts/probe_fit_probes.py`. Inference: non-overlapping
episode-grouped 10-fold, permutation test with
n_perm=2000 recomputing the full cross-validated statistic on shuffled
labels, Holm correction over the 90-test family (latent, pixel and clock
x 5 targets x 6 games), and paired per-fold differences of latent against each baseline.

`latent` is deter+stoch. `pixel` is a fixed random Gaussian projection of the raw frame to
512 dims. `clock` is one-hot quantile bins of the within-episode step index. All three are
scored on identical folds, so the differences are paired.

## What changed relative to the paper's Table 12 and Table 13

1. **Reward proximity inverts.** On all six games the raw frame decodes an imminent level
   clear better than the latent, and every paired difference excludes zero. The paper reads
   these rows as latent structure; they are pixel structure the latent partly discards.
2. **Level identity holds and is now a paired test.** The latent beats pixels with an
   interval excluding zero on vc33, lf52, tn36 and sb26.
3. **The clock is not inert.** Besides decoding elapsed time perfectly, it passes on level
   identity for cd82, ls20, tn36 and sb26. The paper's claim that the clock never passes
   control is false.
4. **sb26 replaces cd82 as the sharp exhibit.** sb26 has the strongest latent level-identity
   decode (0.80, above pixels) and scores zero under every arm including the state-change
   bonus; cd82's zero was removed by that bonus.
